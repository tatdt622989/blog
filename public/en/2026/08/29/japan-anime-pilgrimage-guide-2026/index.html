2026 Japan Anime Pilgrimage Guide: Six Routes, Transport Tips, and Photo Etiquette

An anime pilgrimage in Japan can turn an ordinary sightseeing trip into something much more personal. You may wake up early for a station platform that appeared for only a few seconds, then discover a shopping street or regional dish you would never have noticed on a standard Tokyo–Kyoto itinerary.

The Anime Tourism Association has published its 2026 edition of “88 Japanese Anime Spots.” Alongside long-running favorites, the new list adds destinations connected with Zatsu Tabi: That’s Journey, Love Live! Superstar!!, Maebashi Witches, Initial D, and The Fragrant Flower Blooms with Dignity. Rather than squeezing more than a hundred pins into one impossible itinerary, this guide focuses on six representative routes and shows how to plan them responsibly.

Understanding the 2026 Anime Spot 88 List

“Anime Spot 88” is the name of an annual program, not a promise that the map will always contain exactly 88 coordinates. One title may be associated with several prefectures, while one city may host locations from multiple works. The official 2026 selection therefore contains far more individual regional and facility entries.

Do not choose a destination solely by the popularity of its series. Filter the list using three practical questions:

  1. How dense is the transport network? Walkable locations near frequent trains are best for a first pilgrimage.
  2. Is there official local support? Municipal maps and tourism guides make locations easier to identify and reduce the risk of entering private property.
  3. Is there a good backup plan? If it rains, a facility closes, or a limited campaign has ended, the town should still offer food, culture, scenery, or hot springs.

The six routes below cover everything from an easy half-day stop to a multi-day regional road trip.

Route One: Shinagawa, Tokyo, for a First Pilgrimage

One of the new 2026 entries for The Fragrant Flower Blooms with Dignity is Maxell Aqua Park Shinagawa in Minato City. It is only a short walk from the Takanawa exit of Shinagawa Station, making it easy to add between a Shinkansen journey, an airport transfer, or a day of conventional Tokyo sightseeing.

This route is low risk because the aquarium has clear visitor information and does not require wandering around a residential neighborhood with screenshots in hand. Check the official access and opening information before visiting and allow at least one or two hours inside. If you have more time, continue on the Yamanote Line toward Shibuya for locations associated with Jellyfish Can’t Swim in the Night or Love Live! Superstar!!, but avoid trying to cross all of Tokyo in a single afternoon.

Best for: first-time visitors, families, and travelers with a spare half day.

Route Two: A Full Day of Love Live! Superstar!! in Ushiku

Ushiku entered the 2026 list through Love Live! Superstar!!. The city’s official location guide compares anime frames with photographs of Ushiku Station, Ushiku Daibutsu, Ushiku Chateau, and Kashiwada Shrine, so newcomers do not need to rely on unverified fan maps.

A practical sequence is Ushiku Station → Ushiku Chateau → Ushiku Daibutsu. The station and chateau can be combined on foot. The giant Buddha requires a separate bus journey, and schedules should be checked on the day of travel. If the return connection is tight, setting aside money for a taxi is often more sensible than spending the afternoon worrying about the last bus.

Kashiwada Shrine is an active place of worship, not a film set. Do not block the approach, stage a large photo shoot, or interfere with people who have come to pray. Limited merchandise and stamp campaigns also expire, so always confirm current details with the city or organizer instead of assuming that an old travel report remains valid.

Route Three: Two Different Sides of Gunma

Gunma offers two very different pilgrimage styles. Spend the first day walking from JR Maebashi Station with the official Maebashi Witches visitor information. The station, central shopping streets, Luna Park, and participating businesses can be explored on foot and by local transport. Buying a meal or souvenir from a shop that actively welcomes fans is also a meaningful way to support the host community.

Treat Initial D as a separate second day. Gunma’s official tourism site updated its Haruna and Akagi pilgrimage feature in 2026, but the mountain roads are not part of the same walkable area as central Maebashi. A rental car is practical for Ikaho’s stone steps, Lake Haruna, and the surrounding scenery. Travelers without mountain or winter-driving experience should use buses and choose a smaller area.

An anime pilgrimage is not an invitation to imitate street racing. Use legal parking, watch for residents and cyclists, and stay off mountain roads during fog, ice, fatigue, or severe weather.

Route Four: Choose Only One Tohoku Area from Zatsu Tabi

Zatsu Tabi: That’s Journey is one of the most prominent additions to the 2026 list. It spans Aomori, Iwate, Miyagi, Fukushima, and several other prefectures. The anime’s official location map includes Aizuwakamatsu, Matsushima, Hakkoda, and Shin-Hanamaki, but these places are much farther apart than the single label “Tohoku” suggests.

A realistic breakdown looks like this:

  • Fukushima, one or two days: base yourself in Aizuwakamatsu and combine the castle town with an onsen.
  • Miyagi, one day: travel from Sendai to Matsushima, then walk the coast and Oshima island.
  • Iwate, one or two days: connect Shin-Hanamaki, the Miyazawa Kenji Museum, and the Hanamaki onsen area.
  • Aomori, at least two days: Hakkoda is highly dependent on weather, season, and limited bus schedules.

The JR East location stamp rally promoted for the series ended in 2025. Do not present old campaign details as a current benefit. The better lesson from the series is to allow room for an unexpected detour while still confirming your final train and accommodation in advance.

Route Five: Haikyu!! Monuments Across Sendai

Sendai has mature official information and a useful subway network. As of August 2026, the official Sendai Tourism Ambassador site lists four groups of character monuments:

  • Shoyo Hinata and Tobio Kageyama at Kamei Arena Sendai, near Tomizawa Station.
  • Kei Tsukishima and Tadashi Yamaguchi at the Sendai City Museum.
  • Toru Oikawa and Hajime Iwaizumi at Motoyama Engineering Works Aoba Arena.
  • Takanobu Aone and Kenji Futakuchi at Akiu Sato Center, added in 2026.

The first three can form a city-based day. Akiu deserves another half day or full day, ideally combined with the hot-spring area. Racing through four transfers merely to collect photographs misses the point; Sendai Castle, local food, and an onsen provide the regional context that turns a checklist into a journey.

Route Six: The Ten ONE PIECE Statues of Kumamoto

The ONE PIECE Kumamoto Reconstruction Project is more than a temporary collaboration. Kumamoto Prefecture and the manga created ten Straw Hat Crew statues across areas affected by the 2016 earthquakes, linking visitor spending with the region’s continuing recovery. The official project site provides locations and current announcements.

The statues are spread across Kumamoto City, Aso, Ozu, Nishihara, Mashiki, Minamiaso, Takamori, Mifune, and Uto. Attempting to collect all ten in one day by public transport means spending most of the trip waiting for connections. A more enjoyable plan is:

  1. Begin in Kumamoto City with the Luffy statue outside the prefectural office and nearby city attractions.
  2. Rent a car on day two for Aso and Minamiaso, selecting three to five statues and stopping at local restaurants.
  3. Use day three for Uto or Mifune depending on your onward route, without treating complete collection as an exam.

Before renting, confirm license translation requirements, insurance, volcano notices, and road conditions. If you are uncomfortable driving on the left or on mountain roads, use a current licensed tour rather than forcing an overly ambitious self-drive plan.

A Reliable Planning Method

Start with Official Maps

Social posts are useful for inspiration, but they may show removed displays, construction zones, or private homes. Build your list from the anime’s website, municipal pages, and local tourism associations. Save each location as essential, nearby, or rainy-day backup.

Plan by Time, Not Distance

Two rural locations may be only ten kilometers apart while their bus runs once every two hours. Include waiting, transfers, meals, and photography in your estimate, and identify the final connection back before adding optional stops. On an inter-prefectural travel day, choose no more than one main pilgrimage area.

Save Key Information Offline

Keep tickets, hotel addresses, offline maps, and official location guides on your phone. IC cards such as Suica and PASMO are convenient in major cities, but smaller buses, ferries, and shops may still require cash.

Before departure, use a language tutor or voice assistant to practice polite questions about photography, schedules, and directions. The goal is not a long memorized speech; it is being able to ask permission and confirm the last bus clearly.

Photography and Community Etiquette

The Anime Tourism Association and JNTO repeatedly emphasize that visitors are entering real communities. Problems most often arise around railway crossings, schools, homes, shrines, and ordinary businesses rather than formal attractions.

  • Never enter private property or school grounds. Appearing in a work does not grant public access.
  • Stay out of roads, crossings, and platform edges. No composition is worth endangering yourself or another traveler.
  • Ask before photographing people, shops, or religious interiors. Put the camera away when photography is prohibited.
  • Do not occupy a location with large equipment. Take your photo efficiently and keep resident and business access clear.
  • Keep public transport quiet. Use headphones and do not loudly reenact dialogue inside a train.
  • Carry your rubbish and support local businesses. Meals, accommodation, and local products do more for a destination than a large folder of photos.

Final 2026 Pre-Trip Checklist

One week before departure, verify the following:

  • Official location pages and facility opening days are still current.
  • Limited campaigns have not ended and do not require advance booking.
  • Rural buses, ropeways, ferries, and the final return service are operating.
  • Weather, road closures, earthquakes, and volcano alerts do not affect the route.
  • Your hotel is genuinely close to the first stop on the following morning.
  • You have a battery pack, offline map, spare camera battery, and some cash.
  • Every day includes time to experience the town without looking through a camera.

See the Place, Not Only the Frame

A good anime pilgrimage is not measured by how many screenshots you reproduce from the same angle. Its value comes from allowing a beloved work to introduce you to a city you would otherwise have passed by. The 2026 list offers many entry points, from an easy afternoon in Shinagawa or Ushiku to longer journeys through Tohoku, Sendai, Gunma, and Kumamoto.

Choose one series you genuinely care about, then select a region that fits your transport experience. Keep the schedule loose, respect the people who live there, and confirm current information through official sources. What you find will be more than a background image; it will be a living place with a story beyond the screen.
