## 5 Essential Black Friday Rules for Shoppers in Taiwan

Getting the best headline discount means little if hidden fees wipe out your savings at checkout or customs. Keep these five Taiwan-specific safeguards on hand:

### 1. Respect the NT$2,000 Duty Threshold and 6-Parcel Cap

Under [Taiwan Customs Administration](https://web.customs.gov.tw/) rules, an overseas parcel is duty-free only when **two conditions both hold**: the customs value is **NT$2,000 or less**, and you have used **no more than 6 duty-free imports in the current half-year** (January–June or July–December).

- **Customs value includes shipping**: Customs calculates **item price + international shipping + insurance**. Exceeding NT$2,000 triggers full taxation on the entire parcel (import duty plus 5% VAT), not just the amount above NT$2,000. Most PC parts and electronics carry a 0% tariff rate, leaving only 5% VAT, which still leaves substantial Black Friday savings.
- **Check your remaining quota before buying**: If you ordered cross-border packages during 11.11, open **EZ WAY** or the Customs **e-Clearance App** first. Starting on your seventh parcel between July 1 and December 31, even an NT$300 order is taxed. Splitting orders from the same sender arriving on the same day will also be merged by customs.

### 2. Wireless Gear and the NCC NT$750 Inspection Fee Update

Bluetooth headphones, wireless keyboards, game controllers, and Wi-Fi routers fall under Taiwan’s Class 2 controlled RF devices. Good news for Black Friday shoppers: **the controversial NT$750 per-item NCC review fee for personal overseas parcels was invalidated by the Legislative Yuan and is not being collected**. Standard personal-use quantity limits (typically up to two units) and normal **EZ WAY** customs confirmation still apply.

### 3. Always Pay in Foreign Currency—Refuse DCC

At checkout on Amazon, Shopbop, or hotel sites, you will often see a convenient button offering to charge your card in **TWD**. **Always decline it.** That option is Dynamic Currency Conversion (DCC), and its hidden FX markup commonly adds 3% to 5% on top of bank rates. Pay in **USD or JPY** directly using a Taiwan credit card that earns **2.5%–3%+ on overseas spend** so you more than offset the standard 1.5% foreign transaction fee.

### 4. Know the 120V (US) and 100V (Japan) vs. 110V (Taiwan) Line

- **Safe to import**: Laptops, phones, consoles, headphones, electric toothbrushes, and USB-C gadgets almost universally support **100–240V** dual voltage.
- **Think twice**: High-wattage heating or motor appliances such as espresso machines, hair dryers, rice cookers, and steam ovens. Running 100V Japanese heating units on Taiwan’s 110V outlets can shorten component life, and 120V US models can underperform—plus local repair parts are rarely covered under warranty.

### 5. Set Calendar Reminders on Every Subscription the Minute You Pay

Adobe, Proton, NordVPN, Surfshark, Hostinger, and Coursera Plus all price Black Friday promos as **introductory first-term rates** that auto-renew at double or triple the price. Immediately drop a reminder on your phone calendar **seven days before renewal** so next November brings no surprise charges.
