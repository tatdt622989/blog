Which AI frontend coding subscription should you choose? After collecting plan prices, usage conditions and model rankings in my comparison project, my practical takeaway is simple: **choose a workflow that works for you, then decide whether you need more capacity.**

This is the **September 2026 edition** of an ongoing AI coding subscription comparison series. Each edition will focus on representative plans and changes in pricing, allowances and practical choices. This article preserves its dated snapshot so future editions can be compared against it.

<!--more-->

## Two changes to check on September 14

**Claude Code's weekly allowance is scheduled to fall by about 17% relative to the current promotion.** Starting September 14, the announced level for Pro, Max, Team and seat-based Enterprise is 125% of the original standard, replacing the promotional 150%. That is 25% above the old baseline but roughly 17% below the promotion. The announcement concerns Claude Code weekly limits; it does not establish a uniform cut across every Claude product or usage window. [Official announcement](https://x.com/ClaudeDevs/status/2093742321473065266), [official clarification](https://x.com/ClaudeDevs/status/2093742322525810912)

I have therefore marked Claude's multiplier as **pending reassessment after the change**. The underlying research mixes dates and conditions, so multiplying every old figure by 83% would not produce a new measured result. The post does not specify an exact switchover time; check your account's usage page.

The other change is the pause on new ChatGPT Pro $200 subscriptions, explained below. **Check availability and the revised allowance before choosing a plan.**

The multiplier describes usage valued at the same model's API rates relative to the subscription fee. **It does not measure how much better a model is, or how many more websites it can build.** Claude and ChatGPT estimates draw on different usage records, not a controlled frontend task comparison.

One purchasing update matters: **new subscriptions and upgrades to ChatGPT Pro $200 have been paused since September 10**. Existing subscribers are unaffected, and the $100 tier remains available. ## At $20, choose your main workflow

Claude Pro is a starting point if you intend to use Claude Code for component changes, layout work and understanding a repository. Its monthly price is $20 and includes Claude Code. Establish whether the workflow suits your project before paying for more capacity. [Claude pricing](https://claude.com/pricing)

ChatGPT Plus is another $20 starting point if you already use ChatGPT regularly and want to bring Codex into your work. A higher historical estimate does not settle the decision. Less repeated explanation and less rework can matter more than a higher estimated multiplier. [ChatGPT Plus details](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/6950777), [plan comparison](https://chatgpt.com/pricing/)

Try a real task, such as making an existing page usable on mobile while preserving its interactions. Check whether the tool understands the current code, changes unrelated behavior, or leaves substantial cleanup. **That gives you a more useful measure of everyday cost than copying a model ranking.**

## On a smaller budget, consider OpenCode Go

OpenCode Go costs $10 per month. It is worth comparing if you want to keep spending low and are comfortable switching models. The selected scenarios in my dataset fall around 1.5–6×, but that does not mean every model provides six times the value or unlimited usage.

The official documentation specifies five-hour, weekly and monthly limits. Someone making occasional component changes and someone concentrating development into a weekend may hit very different constraints. A shorter window can interrupt work while monthly capacity remains.

Some higher values also depend on a promotion. Treat those as temporary scenarios, not permanent benefits. Before subscribing, check your intended model, its limits and whether additional balance spending is enabled. [OpenCode Go documentation](https://opencode.ai/docs/go/)

## Include Qoder and WorkBuddy in the shortlist

For the international plans, Qoder Pro includes **2,000 credits for $20**, or **$0.01 per credit**. WorkBuddy Pro includes **1,000 base plus 1,000 monthly bonus credits for $10**, or **$0.005 per credit**. Different deduction rules mean this does **not** establish that WorkBuddy provides twice the usable capacity. [Qoder pricing](https://docs.qoder.com/account/pricing), [WorkBuddy pricing](https://www.workbuddy.ai/docs/workbuddy/pricing)

Using Qoder's official median of seven credits per Editor Agent request at 50K context, Pro's allowance works out to roughly **285 requests**. That is a workload scenario, not a hands-on measurement or guaranteed count. [Official credit guide](https://docs.qoder.com/Credits)

Eligible September first-time Qoder Pro purchases receive **4,000 first-month credits**. WorkBuddy's daily rewards require qualifying client activity; assume neither promotion is a permanent allowance. [Qoder promotion](https://docs.qoder.com/events/bogo), [WorkBuddy terms](https://www.workbuddy.ai/docs/workbuddy/Subscription#limited-time-daily-activity-reward-terms)

For this edition, compare their prices and workflows while leaving their API-equivalent multipliers unassigned. Reliable per-model token conversion is still missing.

## A large multiplier is not an API credit balance

An older research estimate such as 57× values a particular workload at official API rates and divides that amount by the subscription fee. **It does not mean a $20 subscription gives you a freely spendable API balance.**

Results depend on model choice, caching, task length and usage frequency. The source records also span different dates. In particular, the older Claude figures cannot guarantee post-September 14 results.

I would use a high multiplier as a reason to investigate. Renewal should depend on actual use and time saved on corrections and waiting. Separately billed official API usage should not be assumed to come with a subscription.

## When does a $100-plus plan make sense?

I would consider upgrading when the lower tier repeatedly interrupts useful work: a task stops while you wait for a reset, or several ongoing jobs keep running into limits.

Before the change, Claude Pro and the two Max tiers had similar central estimates per dollar. Revised capacity still needs observation. For purchasing, remember: **a larger subscription primarily buys more capacity, without necessarily improving value per dollar.**

Track three things for a month: what you completed, how often limits interrupted you, and how much cleanup you did yourself. If capacity routinely goes unused, a ranking is a weak reason to upgrade. If waiting slows delivery, compare the higher tiers currently available.

For model capability, consult the [Arena Frontend leaderboard](https://arena.ai/leaderboard/code/webdev/frontend), then judge against your own layout, interaction and maintenance requirements. This article presents collected data and a selection approach; it does not claim hands-on testing of every plan.
