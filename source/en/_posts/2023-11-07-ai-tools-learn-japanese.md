The cheapest way to learn Japanese with AI is the **voice mode of a general chatbot**: ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude all hold spoken conversations in Japanese, and a free account is enough to start practicing speaking, listening, writing corrections and grammar questions. Add a dedicated app such as **Aoi** or **TalkMe** only when you want **pronunciation scoring, structured lessons or ready-made scenarios**. Below are the tools still worth using as of October 2026, followed by copy-paste prompts for reading, listening, speaking, writing and the JLPT.

- **Claude**: voice mode is a beta on every plan, including Free; languages other than English are still in beta. Switch to Japanese under **Settings → General → Voice → Language** ([Claude Help](https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11101966-use-voice-mode)).

## Which one to pick

| Your situation | Start with |
| --- | --- |
| Zero budget, just testing | ChatGPT or Gemini voice plus the prompts below |
| You freeze and don't know what to say | Aoi or TalkMe (scenarios and reply hints) |
| You need daily reminders and are starting from kana | Duolingo |
| N3 or above, working on naturalness and keigo | Claude or ChatGPT for rewrites plus voice practice |
| Preparing for the JLPT | Official sample questions plus AI explanations (see below) |

Use one general AI for two weeks first. If you are still speaking every day, then decide whether a paid app is worth it.

## Reading: turn any article into graded material

NHK's simplified-Japanese news site [NEWS WEB EASY](https://news.web.nhk/news/easy/) is a good source, as is anything you already read in Japanese. Paste it into the chat with:

```text
I'm a JLPT N4 learner and my native language is English.
For the Japanese text below:
1. List words above N4 level with their hiragana reading and English meaning.
2. Pick 2 key grammar points, explain each in one sentence, and give one new example.
3. Ask me 3 comprehension questions in Japanese. Wait for my answers before giving the answers and explanations.
(paste text here)
```

## Listening: generate a shadowing script

```text
Write a 150-character everyday dialogue in Japanese at JLPT N3 level
(convenience store or part-time job interview, your choice).
Give me only the Japanese, no translation. After I write down what I heard,
I'll paste it here; mark every line where I misheard or missed something.
```

Then switch to voice mode, ask the AI to read it slowly ("ゆっくり読んでください"), and **shadow** along. AI voices do not always get pitch accent right, so check key words in the University of Tokyo's [OJAD accent dictionary](https://www.gavo.t.u-tokyo.ac.jp/ojad/).

## Speaking: role-play first, corrections after

Send this before starting voice mode:

```text
Let's practice spoken Japanese. My level is N4.
You are a staff member at an izakaya in Tokyo and I am a customer.
My task: order food and ask for a recommendation.
Rules:
- Reply in simple Japanese, 1-2 sentences at a time.
- Don't interrupt my mistakes. When we finish, list my errors in English
  with the correct version and a more natural alternative.
- When I say "ヒント", give me 2 useful words, not a full sentence.
```

English speakers often drop particles or overuse あなた; ask the AI to flag these specifically if they are your habits.

## Writing: journal corrections with a keigo pass

```text
Here is my Japanese journal entry. Please:
1. Give the corrected version.
2. Make a table with columns: original / correction / reason (in English).
3. Rewrite it once in です・ます form and once in respectful keigo, as if speaking to a manager.
(paste entry here)
```

## JLPT prep: let AI explain, use official questions

The next JLPT is on **Sunday, December 6, 2026** ([JLPT official site](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/)). AI-written questions can have the wrong format or wrong answers, so practice with the [official sample questions](https://www.jlpt.jp/e/samples/forlearners.html) and published mock tests, and use AI to **explain mistakes** and **track weak points**:

```text
This is a JLPT N3 grammar question. I chose B; the answer is C.
Explain why C is right and B is wrong, compare the two grammar points,
then give me 3 similar practice questions without the answers.
(paste question here)
```

In the last month before the test, ask the AI each week to turn your list of wrong answers into review cards for your five most-missed grammar points.

## Three things to watch

1. **AI makes mistakes**, especially with keigo nuance, dialects and kanji readings. Check anything important in a dictionary or textbook.
2. **Set the output format**: state your level, native language and the format you want, or the AI will over-explain or mix languages.
3. **A fixed daily slot beats more tools**: 15 minutes of voice practice every day does more than three overlapping subscriptions.
